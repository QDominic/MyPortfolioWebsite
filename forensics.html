  Digital Forensics

Uncovering the Truth Digitally

Digital forensics bridges the gap between cybercrime and justice — recovering evidence that exists only in the digital realm.

How Digital Forensics Solves Crimes

Most individuals are aware of forensic science, where physical evidence is collected from a crime scene. Digital forensics is different — the "crime scene" exists inside computerised devices. Due to the global nature of digital crime, professionals in this field are in high demand across criminal investigations worldwide.

What it's used for

Investigating Computers, Phones & Embedded Systems

Professionals examine smartphones, computers, and embedded devices when they may contain evidence relating to a suspect's whereabouts, communications, web activity, and app usage — in both physical and cybercrime cases.

How it works

GPS, Files, Hacker Signatures & Network Trails

A forensic investigator can use a suspect's phone GPS to verify location, examine source code to trace a hacker's methods, or follow the digital trail left behind during a network attack. Every attacker leaves a signature.

Cybercrime investigations

Starting at the Victim's Device

In cybercrime cases, investigations begin on the victim's computer or network. Forensic specialists discover the origin and nature of the attack, then follow the hacker's signature trail to damning documents and devices.

Act 1038

Ghana's Cybersecurity Act, 2020 establishing the CSA

Evidence in the Cloud Age

Agencies must do more than just seize physical devices. Forensic examiners retrieve data from seized hardware — but increasingly, critical evidence resides on cloud-based storage services. This requires specialised knowledge of cloud architectures and legal frameworks to properly acquire and preserve digital evidence.

The Cyber Security Authority (CSA) of Ghana was established under the Cybersecurity Act, 2020 (Act 1038) to regulate cybersecurity in Ghana, promote its development, and protect citizens. Protection of Children Online is a key priority — our children are Ghana's future.
